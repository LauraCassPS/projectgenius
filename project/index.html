Why this matters

Most businesses don't have an operations problem. They have an operations debt.

The systems were built for the business you were two years ago. The spreadsheets multiplied. The workarounds became the process. The team learned to work around the cracks instead of through them.

It still works. Until it doesn't.

By the time a CEO calls me, the cost isn't just inefficiency. It's the hours lost to admin no one wants to do. The decisions delayed because the data lives in five places. The team stretched thin because nothing scales without them in the room.

The hidden costs of operations debt

  1. 01 Hours on admin no one wants to own
  2. 02 Decisions delayed because the data lives in five places
  3. 03 Growth bottlenecked on the leader being in the room

Operations debt compounds quietly. The fix is rarely more tools. It's the right tools, built the right way, around how the business actually runs.

How it works

Three movements.
One system the team actually uses.

Every engagement follows the same path. Map what's really happening. Build what the business needs. Train the team to run it without me.

  1. 01 — MAP

    The business as it actually runs.

    Before a single tool is configured, I map how the work moves through your business today. The official process and the real one. Where decisions stall. Where data goes missing. Where the team has built workarounds the leadership doesn't know about.

    This is the work most consultants skip. It's also the reason the system holds up later.

    DeliverablesStakeholder interviews, process map document, gap analysis

  2. 02 — BUILD

    The right tools, configured properly.

    Then I build the system in ClickUp, Monday.com, Notion, or whatever fits the business best. Workflows, automations, integrations. Not a template dropped in and handed over, but a system shaped around how your team actually works.

    The tools matter less than the build. Most operations problems aren't a software problem.

    DeliverablesConfigured workspace, automations, internal documentation

  3. 03 — TRAIN

    Handover is where most projects fail.

    I train your team to use the system, adapt it, and own it. The engagement isn't done when the build is live. It's done when the team can run it, change it, and grow into it without me in the room.

    This is what makes the work sustainable. It's also why clients come back.

    DeliverablesTrained team, handover sessions, 30-day support window

About

Portrait of Kate

The operator behind the work.

I've spent over twenty years inside other people's businesses, working out why things aren't running the way they should and building the systems that fix it.

I started in operations roles inside growing companies, then moved into consulting. For the last ten years I've worked independently, mostly with clients who found me through referrals and word of mouth. The work has taken me from a Formula 1 event in Singapore to a global coffee chain to a luxury patisserie working with international fashion houses, and dozens of growing businesses in between.

What I do hasn't changed in twenty years. I map how a business actually runs. I build the systems it needs. I train the team to use them.

What has changed is who I work with. The clients I do my best work for now are leaders who want infrastructure to match how fast they're growing, and who'd rather pay for it once, properly, than keep patching it.

I work with clients globally.
